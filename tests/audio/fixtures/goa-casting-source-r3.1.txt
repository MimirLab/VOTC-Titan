GOA2 / VOTC — BREEZE TTS 2 ACOUSTIC VOICE DESIGN
Revision r3.1, 2026-10-14 — direct acoustic casting correction. Prompt specification for integration.

This document governs how an already written line sounds. Its companion GOA2-TEXT-MODEL-DIALOGUE-STEERING-r3.txt governs what the character says. The user currently has no implemented linguistic or dialect-script steering from this proposal. The records below are authored acoustic directions for a future implementation, not a report of an existing feature.

Breeze synthesizes the supplied text. It can render a low resonant voice, a Jamaican accent, clipped phrasing, stress on explicitly marked existing words, and a laugh that the upstream writer already chose. It does not decide to say “ja mon,” write a joke, set a bargain price, choose an oath, express a worldview, select a religious invocation, or decide that an event calls for laughter. Those choices belong to the text model. Accent changes pronunciation and timing of existing words; dialect-script steering changes vocabulary, grammar, idiom, and discourse. They are separate responsibilities.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT CONTAINS

The anatomy catalogue retains 115 profiles and the race routing catalogue retains 92 exact native race traits. The culture catalogue contains 166 usable native culture keys, each with one acoustic paragraph. The sole dummy key wc_dummy has no voice tack. The 35 faith delivery records retain their exact tack IDs and faith aliases, but they are inactive until upstream explicitly chooses a delivery record for a line. These instructions extend the prior synthetic worldbuilding through sound, while preserving the boundary between researched casting analogies and authored extrapolation.

The body profiles are casting suggestions: ordinary adults of the specified presentation, not mandatory pitches for every individual. Actual age, form, impairment, and established voice identity take precedence over a generic default. A character's temperament and competence never follow automatically from body timbre. Culture determines learned articulation and cadence; anatomy determines the physical register and resonance. Adopting Black Dragon culture does not give a human a dragon throat. A dragon wearing a human visage needs the current form's voice with the same learned accent.

THE UPSTREAM INTERFACE

The following is a proposed internal application contract, not an existing Breeze API schema. Upstream supplies literal_text, character_voice_id or an approved reference WAV with its exact transcript, a resolved anatomy_profile_id, an explicitly selected culture_acoustic_id, optional explicitly selected faith_delivery_id, and delivery_metadata. Delivery metadata may include pace, emotion, intensity, volume, existing-word emphasis spans, pause positions, and vocal events already chosen by the writer. The adapter maps these to the selected backend's real text, instruction, reference, and sampling fields.

The literal spoken words remain the writer's words. The adapter must not invent, remove, paraphrase, reorder, or repeat them. Any documented vocal-event marker is placed in the TTS rendering by an explicit upstream event request. For example, the writer can supply “That was your plan? (laugh) Then we should try the other door.” Breeze renders that chosen laugh. No race, culture, or religion lookup independently inserts it. A plain line without a chosen laugh stays a plain line.

Existing emphasis spans must identify words or offsets in literal_text. They do not ask Breeze to choose which names, prices, sacred terms, threats, or jokes deserve emphasis. Culture's generic stress contours operate over the supplied phrase structure; a scene-specific stress target comes from explicit upstream metadata. Faith records operate over the supplied phrases and marked positions. They select neither religious vocabulary nor conditions of activation.

Breeze receives only the acoustic instruction, the literal line, and its reference conditioning. It does not need the character biography, scene analysis, religion doctrine, lore book, synthetic cultural worldview, event eligibility lists, or habit scheduler. Those records belong upstream. An implementation may retain them for the text model, but they are not part of the TTS instruction.

VOICE DESIGN, CLONE, AND DIRECTION

Official Voice Design creates a new voice from a natural-language instruction without reference audio. Voice Clone uses reference audio and its exact complete transcript without an instruction. Voice Direction uses the same reference plus a delivery instruction. Official guidance recommends matching instruction language to the target text and using CFG 4 to strengthen design or direction. Clean non-looping reference speech with minimal background noise is the documented reference input. English and Chinese are the published speech languages.

Design each character's synthetic voice once. Compose its anatomy core, one culture acoustic paragraph, and bounded individual differences in register, grain, or phrase rhythm. Generate candidate references using a fixed documented audition text, select the reference that satisfies the casting brief, and retain the clean WAV, exact transcript, prompt, seed, model identity, and codec identity. A proposed audition length around 10–20 seconds is a practical starting choice, not an official requirement. No candidates have been generated or auditioned for this specification.

Subsequent lines use the saved reference to retain speaker identity and a concise Voice Direction instruction to render the explicitly chosen delivery. A seed reproduces a synthesis trajectory under matching conditions; it is not a speaker identifier across different texts. Persist character ID to reference mapping across sessions. Use campaign identity as part of the record key so an unrelated campaign character does not accidentally inherit a cached voice.

Do not rewrite the speaker design every turn or stack every lore feature into the TTS prompt. The initial design paragraph establishes the voice. Reference-conditioned directions then control how that speaker delivers the supplied line. When a strong accent change is explicitly desired, audition and retain a derived reference; cloned-reference direction is designed to protect timbre and may preserve the old accent strongly. Current physical transformations may require a paired reference rather than a minor delivery instruction.

Sex and form are explicit routing inputs where anatomy differs. Naga entry 030 has separate male and female cores; their difference is much more than pitch. Male naga use very low broad chest-and-throat resonance and controlled reptilian guttural grain. Female naga retain smoother, ringing elven-derived resonance and a finer sibilant edge. Both can carry the same Nazja cultural articulation. The dryad/keeper alias uses entry 052 for the female dryad form and entry 053 for the male keeper form. Sayaadi entry 092 distinguishes incubus and succubus presentation. A culture key never resolves these physical routes by itself.

For native constraints, null presentation entries remain null. They are not accidental missing defaults. An unusual presentation in the custom fork requires an explicitly authored compatibility choice upstream. Do not silently assign another race's body. The native Banshee restriction and the Dryad/Keeper alias are distinct rules.

ACOUSTIC COMPOSITION

For a new voice, begin with current physical form and individual presentation. Add exactly one dominant culture accent and cadence. A hybrid culture may contribute one secondary rhythmic feature if explicitly selected and persisted upstream. Optional faith prosody joins only when upstream chose it. Scene delivery can soften or intensify those prosodic tendencies while retaining the speaker. Upstream determines precedence; Breeze is not asked to infer a religion scene or solve character identity conflicts.

Culture paragraphs below deliberately contain phonetic and rhythmic guidance. They contain no culturally preferred utterances, merchant bargaining moves, worldview statements, ritual vocabulary, joke choices, or spontaneous bodily noises. Cultural phonetics remain audible through another anatomy: a Gilnean tauren can have a huge bovine resonator and Cockney vowels, while an elf raised among Darkspear can retain an elven speaker timbre with Jamaican pronunciation.

The accent analogies are casting references, not claims that Azeroth has Earth's languages or that every regional accent is canonically established. Much of the smaller-culture differentiation is creative extrapolation from the prior synthetic corpus. Use the named accent and specified phonetic direction directly in the live acoustic prompt. Preserve source attribution and casting notes outside that prompt. Identical anatomical bodies can still differ in learned culture and individual identity.

Optional register variants such as a Gilnean courtly pronunciation or a dockworker Cockney pronunciation belong to explicit upstream casting metadata. The gilnean catalogue paragraph supplies a Cockney baseline; it does not inspect title, wealth, occupation, or class and autonomously change the accent. The same rule applies to formal versus colloquial pronunciation in other cultures.

EVENTS AND PHYSICAL TEXTURE

The documented English vocal-event examples are (laugh), (cough), (clears throat), and (sigh). The writer supplies whether and where an event occurs, and delivery metadata can specify its acoustic realization. There is no event probability, cooldown, maximum-event scheduler, semantic eligibility classifier, or automatic hm/mm insertion in this Breeze document. Those r2 mechanisms were misplaced and are removed from the acoustic contract.

Timbre can be described as raspy, wet-edged, creaky, ringing, hollow, gravelly, nasal, or guttural. These descriptions request a texture beneath intelligible existing speech. They do not insert a separate hiss, roar, bark, growl, creak, clack, or sound effect. Unsupported event names are not treated as documented control tokens. A quiet or noisy scene is the upstream writer's explicit delivery choice.

Repeatable supernatural processing such as spectral doubling, strong reverberation, subharmonics, metallic resonance effects, or layered ghost tones belongs to a separately implemented deterministic audio stage when required. Breeze's text directions provide an acoustic approximation, not an exact effects-control API. Keep reference WAVs clean, and audition any processing before adopting it. No processing implementation or sound-quality result is claimed here.

BACKEND FIELD ADAPTER

Official PyTorch multipart /v1/audio/speech uses text, singular instruction, ref_audio, ref_text, cfg_scale, and seed. HoppouAI native forms additionally support voice_id and sampling controls. Its OpenAI-compatible JSON uses input, plural instructions, voice as a saved ID or an object containing id, response_format, and stream_format; cfg_scale and seed remain extra JSON fields. HoppouAI documents speed as ignored, so speaking pace belongs in the direction instruction.

The remominor JSON examples use input, instructions, voice such as clone:sky, guidance_scale, seed, response_format, stream, and stream_format. Its multipart examples use text, instruction, ref_audio, ref_text, cfg_scale, seed, and response_format. These wrappers have different field conventions; the adapter must target the actual selected server. Nothing in this document assumes that the user's current server is any specific one of them.

HoppouAI registers references through POST /v1/voices with ref_audio and ref_text; adding name saves a persistent named voice. Its .breeze reference cache includes encoded audio and transcript but is codec-dependent; retain the original WAV as the durable source. remominor documents reusable profiles through POST /upload_voice or /v1/upload_voice, and lists them at GET /v1/audio/voices. Read the selected server's actual schema before implementing profile upload.

HoppouAI WebSocket directions can change for future queued pieces while the saved voice remains selected. remominor's experimental continuation retains generation state across complete sequential requests with matching voice, instruction, guidance, and seed. Its retained state is short-lived and is not a durable character voice record. Neither transport decides the script, infers a laugh, or replaces the persistent reference mapping.

LITERAL-LINE EXAMPLES

Literal text for either naga: “You have arrived early. The council will hear you at sunset.” Male design selects anatomy 030 male and culture naga; female design selects anatomy 030 female and the same culture naga. The sentences are identical. Their resonance, throat texture, vowels, and cadence differ through the chosen acoustic profiles.

Literal goblin line: “The agreed price is twelve silver pieces.” Upstream has already chosen every word and the amount. If it marks twelve for emphasis, Breeze stresses twelve in the saved goblin voice with the chosen culture accent. Breeze adds no discount, counteroffer, joke, nickname, or extra sales pitch.

Literal Darkspear line: “I will meet you by the river.” Breeze may pronounce it with the selected Jamaican acoustic paragraph. It does not append “mon,” change the grammar, or invent a loa reference. If the text model later writes an idiom or loa reference, that independently written literal line becomes the synthesis input.

Literal line with an upstream event: “That was your plan? (laugh) Then we should try the other door.” Delivery metadata may specify one brief breathy laugh. Removing the explicitly chosen event marker produces the corresponding spoken line without that laugh; culture does not restore it.
